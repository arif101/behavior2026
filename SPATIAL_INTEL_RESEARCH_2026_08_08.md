# Spatial-intelligence deep research (date: 2026-08-08)

## 1. Landscape verdict

**Latent geometry has converged on our architecture, and then kept going past it.** Three independent 2026 papers reinvent our split — GEAR-VLA (2026) zero-inits a geometry pathway into a frozen semantic visual stream; 3DThinkVLA (2026) explicitly separates low-level geometry alignment from high-level spatial reasoning at different injection depths; "Direct Action-Head Injection of a Grounded 3D Point" (2026) validates our affordance→AdaLN route *on π0.5 itself* (3D-via-AdaLN 77.5 vs 3D-via-text 38.5, and AdaLN 68.9 vs state-concatenation 51.7). Nobody else runs a persistent multi-scale voxel map at 13–21 ms CPU with a staleness channel; SERF (2026) on BEHAVIOR-1K is the only close sibling, and it has two things we lack — the robot's own body as a map channel, and token readouts in *multiple reference frames* rather than world frame only. **4D perception is where the field is loudest and weakest**: every memory retrofit (HAMLET 2026, IntentVLA 2026, µVLA 2026, MemoryVLA 2026) injects history on the prefix/context route that our own G1 measured as consumed-but-unpaid and froze, and HAMLET's only independent reproduction on a π0.5 backbone (NativeMEM 2026) moved 8.8% → 9.0%. **Precision terminal control is the one area where the literature is ahead of us and mechanistically specific**: FACT (2026) diagnoses π0.5's own flow-matching noise schedule as starving the τ<0.2 regime where sub-millimetre corrections are denoised (8.9% of gradient signal), and the 2025 BEHAVIOR challenge winner reports that "close on empty air" is the benchmark-wide dominant failure — our commit problem is the field's commit problem on this exact benchmark. **World-action models are a scoping trap**: the head-to-head robustness study (2026) finds π0.5 at 85.7% on LIBERO-Plus *above* the best world-action model (Cosmos-Policy 82.2%) at ≥4.8× the inference cost, and V-JEPA 2 (2025) plans at ~16 s/action. What we uniquely have and nobody else does is the combination of eval-legal *mid-episode sim-restore* (which makes offline counterfactual labelling free) and a metric affordance point already wired into the winning injection route; what we lack is any ordering/preference objective on our progress head, any use of our failed rollouts, and any calibrated uncertainty readout at the commit boundary.

## 3. Run-3 / multi-task candidates

- **HAMLET-lite temporal mixer over the map-token contract.** HAMLET (ICLR 2026)'s one capacity-controlled result is the useful one: raw concatenation of the same moment tokens gives +0.1 while a 2-layer causal transformer gives +2.8 — *temporal aggregation*, not added capacity, is the mechanism. A 2-layer causal transformer over the last T map-token sets, output shape-preserving (8,72), costs ~124K params and changes nothing downstream. Deviate from the paper twice: log-spaced stride {t, t−4, t−16, t−32} instead of chunk-length stride (our map runs every frame; theirs did not), and route through AdaLN rather than prefix-concat. Skip Stage-1 time-contrastive init — the paper's own ablation prices it at +0.3 to +0.6. **Must beat a twenty-line proprio-history control (A1) that the paper never ran.**
- **Action-conditioning the progress head (Q-form).** Cosmos Policy (2026)'s well-powered ablation (1500 trials/suite) shows the aux value/next-state prediction gain concentrates on long-horizon and goal suites (Long −3.6, Goal −1.5) versus Spatial −0.5, Object −0.2 — a mode-selection effect, not a motion-quality effect. Converting our V-like head to Q-like is a loss/input change on an existing head and turns the de-aliasing aux directly into the chunk-selection critic.
- **QDepth-VLA-style depth-expert branch.** QDepth-VLA (2026) reports the *branch* matters more than the loss (−8.5 for removing the dedicated depth expert vs −3.9 for zeroing the loss), quantized latent depth tokens beat pixel regression by 3.9pp, and their hybrid attention mask is worth 5.5pp. Our GT-depth aux is currently the weak (thin-head) version, and we have sim GT depth rather than their monocular estimates, so the ceiling should be higher. Fully legal: depth is target-only.
- **Terminal-mode gating on L2.** SPHINX (2025) and RVT-2 (2024) both win precision by *re-rendering geometry at higher effective resolution near the target*, and ActiveVLA (2026) prices the zoom component at +2.4pp on RLBench. Our L2 0.02 m wrist cubes are already a foveated zoom that we currently pool away. Concrete: when the stage head fires near-commit, re-run inference with the AdaLN route driven by L2/wrist-local geometry rather than global L0/L1.
- **Anchored-relative action parameterization.** GEAR-VLA (2026) expresses all H future poses relative to the *same* current EE pose rather than step-wise, motivated by within-chunk error accumulation — directly on our 2.2 cm frontier. Converter-side change, no architecture. But it invalidates norm stats and every checkpoint (fresh run), and the paper never ablates it. Low priority against items above.
- **SERF-style multi-reference-frame map tokens.** Evaluated on BEHAVIOR-1K. Ego/gripper-frame readout of L1/L2 alongside world frame is a cheap change to the pooling head and is the framing most likely to help terminal precision. Also: SERF puts the *robot body* in the map; our L2 sees the gripper only incidentally.
- **PEEK-style overlay as an affordance-injection A/B.** PEEK (2026) paints the target path/mask onto the RGB observation, reaching the frozen SigLIP+Gemma stack rather than only the action expert — a genuinely different information path, no architecture change, ablatable against our AdaLN route in days. Its 41.4× figure is for a sim-trained policy, our setting.

## 4. ResFiT & chunk-critic intelligence

**The gating question, first.** Every selection method here — SEAL (2026), V-GPS (2024), VGAS (2026), DREAM-Chunk (2026), and DSRL (2025) explicitly — can only re-rank or re-select within the base policy's support. DSRL steers the *latent noise* input, so it provably cannot create mass the base assigns zero. If §2 item 6 returns B < 1.5·p̂, all of these die together and the budget belongs to corrective data and terminal precision. **SPARR (ICRA 2026) is the exception and this is the single most important structural finding in the survey**: base contributes a *deterministic mean*, residual is additive, and the **sum is clamped to the full normalized action range** — Fig. 8 shows the residual inverting the base's yaw sign. That gives the residual full authority regardless of base support, making ResFiT strictly more capable than the chunk-critic on our stated frontier, and it argues ResFiT should be sequenced **first**, not after the critic.

**But SPARR's precondition is exactly what we lack.** Its stated limitation: if the base achieves near-zero real-world success, the residual is insufficient — their RLPD demo buffer is seeded from ~20 base successes, and stripping that yields SERL, which they measure at near-zero. Our fix is the thing they explicitly could not have: **mid-episode sim-restore manufactures terminal-phase successes at policy-visited states without the base ever succeeding.** That converts their blocking precondition into an engine we already built. Pre-RL GO/NO-GO: ≥40 successful seed trajectories must exist. Check this before launching anything.

**Value target — take Cosmos Policy's, not a hand-designed one.** Cosmos Policy (2026) defines V = γ^(H−t)·R with R ∈ [0,1] explicitly allowed to be a *fractional partial-credit score*. BEHAVIOR's per-episode q is the same object, scored at episode end. So MC targets are free, aligned with the literal scoring rule, and automatically encode "transient progress = 0". Also adopt their **two-checkpoint discipline** (freeze the BC policy, train the critic only on rollouts from that frozen checkpoint) — for us it has a second benefit: a critic reading action-expert features without gradient flow cannot churn the (8,72) contract. **Warning from the same paper: they compared model-free Q(s,a) against model-based V(s') and found Q consistently worse, attributed to sample efficiency under limited rollout data.** Their V(s') wins by laundering the action through a 2B pretrained video world model we cannot build. We are structurally forced into the form they found weaker; the untested mitigation is that our restore-splice data is *concentrated at near-commit states* rather than undirected.

**Objective and architecture details worth copying verbatim:**
- **Pairwise ordering loss** (Robometer, RSS 2026) — but at *frame-pair* level inside the batch, not trajectory level, because our head is a Markovian MLP over one chunk's pooled features and we cannot feed video pairs or reverse clips (reversal invalidates the action labels on the same forward pass). Within-episode rewind-equivalent: sample i<j from the same episode, add −log σ((p̂_j − p̂_i)/temp). ~8 lines. This is the branch guaranteed to have data.
- **Anti-collapse is mandatory.** VGAS (2026) introduces Explicit Geometric Regularization specifically to stop value collapse and preserve ranking resolution among near-miss candidates; WCM (2026) uses SIGReg; DriveSuprim (2025) reports the same pathology independently in driving. Our stage head on action-expert features is an unguarded collapse risk.
- **State-Action Fusion** (VGAS 2026): condition the raw candidate chunk on proprioception *before* mixing with visual tokens, otherwise attention ignores the sparse proprio signal — directly relevant given our (8,72) map tokens plus affordance tokens will drown it.
- **Adaptive prefix length** (ACSAC 2026): a causal-masked critic emits Q for every horizon h in one forward pass, then argmax jointly over candidate *and* prefix length — that is the commit question in its literal form (how far do I commit before re-deciding), and it is the difference between hovering and over-committing. Companion Adaptive Q-Chunking (2026) notes Q values are not comparable across horizons and must be normalized against a per-horizon baseline or the argmax degenerates to longest prefix.
- **Q-chunking (2025)** is the correct foundation (unbiased n-step backups over the chunk, offline-to-online with a behavior constraint against our policy-visited corpus) rather than per-step Q.
- **WCM (2026)** already built this against frozen π0/π0.5: ~107M critic, causal trunk over K frames, value head + action-conditioned next-latent head, sparse binary success supervision sufficient. Adopt the architecture rather than re-deriving it; ignore the 8×H100 training cost claim only after checking whether the LeJEPA trunk is the load-bearing part.
- **Residual observation design** (Object-Centric Residual RL, 2026): the residual observes *low-dimensional object-centric state* (poses + proprio + base action), not pixels — maps 1:1 onto our affordance 3D point. Their **pose-noise injection matched to real estimator error and pose-dropout** is the antidote to our 2.6 cm affordance error; train under noise at our measured scale so the residual degrades gracefully rather than trusting a bad point at commit. Quaternion-multiplicative rotation composition, not additive, for a bimanual arm.
- **Terminal-state quality** (Foresight Residual RL, 2026): per-subtask sparse rewards improve each subtask yet chain to nothing, because terminal-state *quality* is uncontrolled. Their fix — an offline predictor of downstream success from terminal-state images, used as a reward *multiplier* — is reproducible for free with our splice engine, which already restores mid-episode states and rolls forward.
- **Reward-hacking guard, specific to us.** ToggledOn fires on ==5 consecutive contact∧marker-overlap steps and break-and-reestablish toggles it back OFF; scoring is at episode end. A "literal ever fired" reward will train the residual to jitter the button. Pre-register **"literal true at segment end AND held ≥K steps"**. Robometer supplies zero reward-hacking analysis despite being deployed as an RL reward; Cosmos supplies none either.
- **The asymmetry, inverted.** SPARR's rule is: give the residual the channel the base is corrupted or blind on. Our base is RGB-rich but *Markovian and metric-blind*, so the residual gets pooled map tokens + staleness (which encode history the base cannot see), the affordance 3D point, and proprio. Be honest that the stage-head feature is a deterministic function of base features and adds **zero new bits** — include it as a gate, not as an information channel.
- **The Warp RL (2026) counterweight**: additive residuals cannot rescale or reshape the base distribution, and under geometric mismatch can underperform the unadapted base. SPARR dodges this by collapsing the base to a deterministic mean with a full-range clamp. Lock that design choice or we inherit the critique.

**Horizon shaping is not optional.** SPARR's sparse reward lives at H≈50–150 steps; our episodes are 1–2 orders longer. Gate the residual on the commit window, restore into near-commit states, cap segments at ~60 steps. For the *first* experiment gate by **oracle restore-state selection, not the stage head** — stage-head reliability at near-commit is the very aliasing problem under test, and confounding the two makes the result uninterpretable.

## 5. World-action-model synthesis

**Do not build a generative world model. The scoping evidence is unambiguous.** The head-to-head robustness study (2026) puts π0.5 at 85.7% on LIBERO-Plus *above* Cosmos-Policy at 82.2%, with world-action models ≥4.8× slower per inference. V-JEPA 2-AC (2025) plans at ~16 s/action (vs ~4 min for pixel-space Cosmos) — three orders of magnitude off our control budget. Cosmos Policy's own planner costs ~5 s/chunk and forced them to abandon receding-horizon control entirely. And generative-WAM-as-data-source methods (Ctrl-World 2025, WMPO 2025) are strictly dominated for us: OmniGibson is fast and exact, and our sim-restore and stall-kick engines already occupy that slot.

**Also: SEAL's contribution is unavailable to us and this must be stated plainly.** SEAL's dynamics model P̂ is K=10 vectorized copies of the ground-truth eval simulator with state save/restore, and there is **no ablation with any approximate world model anywhere in the paper**. Reading or writing OmniGibson state at eval is privileged-state-illegal for us, and K rendering Isaac envs will not fit in the 24 GB submission budget we proved in G1. The sweep note's premise "we already have the forward-simulation rollout" is right for *offline labelling* and wrong for eval. That distinction decides the line.

**The minimal viable path, in build order, each step usable standalone:**

1. **FK lookahead (free, legal, today).** Our action space is absolute joint targets, so a forward-kinematics rollout of the commanded chunk yields the predicted terminal gripper pose with no physics and no privileged state. Verifier = distance to the affordance point + approach-axis alignment + occupancy/contact in the L2 wrist cube, scored **continuously with argmax** (not SEAL's binary first-accept, whose false positives are executed immediately and show up as per-task regressions throughout their own tables). Sub-millisecond, no API. **Gated on one measurement**: median ‖FK-predicted − achieved‖ terminal error; `probes/chunk_jitter.py` already flags absolute-joint tracking as a live hypothesis, and >5 cm kills this against a 2.2 cm button.
2. **One-step latent dynamics in a space we already pay for.** DINO-WM (2025) predicts frozen DINOv2 patch features one step ahead — and we already run frozen DINO for the affordance head, so the encoder is sunk cost. The better target for us is our own **frozen (8,72) map-token contract**: f((8,72)_t, chunk) → (8,72)_{t+1} is a tiny model over a stable, geometrically meaningful latent, and it is exactly the "compact geometric latent is a better dreaming space than raw pixels" argument. This is the warm start for the action-conditioned progress critic.
3. **DREAM-Chunk's self-supervised verifier as the interim selector.** DREAM-Chunk (2026) needs *no reward labels*: sample N chunks, roll each one's dreamed latent forward, and during execution pick the chunk whose predicted future is actually coming true (min phase-aligned L2 to the observed latent). World-model overhead ~5 ms; already validated on π0.5. It can ship while the critic is still training, and its dynamics module is the same f() from step 2.
4. **FLARE-style coupling as the cheapest training-time WAM.** FLARE (2025) appends M=32 learnable future tokens to the flow-matching DiT and aligns them at layer 6 to an embedding of the observation one chunk ahead — cosine loss, no pixel decoder, **zero inference cost**. Its ablation is the cleanest published isolation of prediction-improves-control (43.9 → 49.6 raw target → 55.0 action-aware target). For us the alignment target should be our map-token contract or frozen-DINO features, so the prediction objective supervises the same features AdaLN already consumes, and the 32 future tokens are the natural mount for the stage head.
5. **Gate the expensive parts on the cheap signal.** Use denoising variance (2026) or the stage head to fire N-sample lookahead only at near-commit states, not every step. N× the π0.5 forward pass is the real budget line.

**Two costs to state honestly.** We cannot do SEAL's interleaved autoregressive loop, because closing it requires predicting *images* to re-feed the policy — so without step 2 working we get **single-chunk open-loop lookahead only**, h32 ≈ 1.07 s, versus SEAL's whole variable-length subgoal. Our unit of verification may be shorter than the commit decision it is meant to score; G1's finding that torso depth is necessary-not-sufficient suggests the descent straddles chunks. And a run-time predictor we rank against must be validated on **contact events specifically** — the WAM failure-mode survey (2026) names "visually plausible futures that violate contact and force closure" as the characteristic error, which is precisely a 2.2 cm button press.

## 6. Threats & humility

**Our own premise is internally inconsistent and I am treating that as the top risk.** The brief says commit-mode mass is "literally zero at near-commit states." `RUN1_G1_REPORT.md` says the opposite in kind: "the initiation wall is probabilistic, not absolute," conversion is a rare mode at ~3–10%/rollout pooled, formation→conversion ran 1/3. The "0/25" figure is a per-config chunk count, not a per-state law. These imply different programs: at p≈5% per *sample at a fixed state*, best-of-32 → 0.81 and the whole selection line is alive; if the 1/3 is upstream state-level luck and resampling at a fixed state is degenerate, best-of-K and DSRL are both worth exactly zero. **Nobody has measured which.** Do not spend a Run-3 slot on either branch until §2 item 6 reports.

**The prefix-token negative is broader and worse than we've been treating it.** Our G1 froze the prefix map-token route as consumed-but-unpaid. "Decoupled Action Expert" (2026) makes it a general law under exactly our conditions: with the backbone *frozen*, cross-attention collapses 61.3 → 19.8 and prefix tokens 56.3 → 19.5 while AdaLN/AdaLN-Zero fully preserve performance. PointACT (2026) votes the same way independently. And **both of the memory papers we like inject on that dead route** — IntentVLA's intent token is sequence-concatenated (the paper explicitly states no AdaLN modulation is applied, contradicting the sweep note's "drop-in for our AdaLN route"), and HAMLET concatenates into the action-expert prefix. HAMLET's only third-party π0.5 reproduction (NativeMEM 2026) got 8.8% → 9.0%. Caveat in the other direction: Decoupled Action Expert benchmarks Diffusion Policy on MimicGen/LIBERO, not a VLA, so verify the collapse on our stack before betting the contract on it.

**The AdaLN route is well supported — for a *low-dimensional, scene-invariant* quantity, which is not what we push through it.** "Direct Action-Head Injection" (2026) Table 3 is the uncomfortable one: displacement alone 68.9 > d + p_target + p_gripper 59.7 > p_target + p_gripper 53.7. **More information through AdaLN was worse.** Our affordance→AdaLN route is the winning arm of that ablation; our map-geometry→AdaLN route is structurally the losing arm. That is a live hypothesis for why the affordance head "produced every task success" and the map has not, and it deserves an explicit ablation rather than an assumption.

**Foveated voxels vs learned latents: the evidence cuts against us in two directions.** GEAR-VLA (2026) measures frozen VGGT at 85.2 vs no VGGT at 85.1 — **a frozen geometry encoder buys nothing**; only a trainable one pays (−3.5 when frozen). Any3D-VLA (2026) independently found VGGT-derived implicit priors worth +1.0pp over a 2D baseline, essentially nothing, and attributes the failure to lack of metric alignment and spatial hallucination "during fine-grained manipulation" — our exact regime. Our (8,72) contract is frozen *by contract*. The counter-argument (our downstream pooling head is trainable; our map is hand-built and metric, not a learned encoder) is real but is an argument by disanalogy, not evidence. Separately, 3D Diffuser Actor (2024) argues a holistic non-tokenized 3D scene embedding underperforms a tokenized one — our aggressive pooling to (8,72) is by construction the holistic kind, and a per-region token variant is the cheap experiment we have never run. Spatial Forcing (2026) reports large gains from the same VGGT features used as an *alignment target* rather than an input feature; that unresolved tension with Any3D-VLA means any geometry-aux we run needs a no-aux control, not an assumption that the published numbers transfer.

**The deepest threat: we may be optimizing an axis we are not failing on.** Every axis the geometry literature measures — unseen objects, backgrounds, lighting, camera viewpoint, embodiment — is appearance/embodiment robustness. Our measured failure is decision aliasing at commit. Better geometry improves *where* the policy thinks the target is; the affordance head already solves that at 2.6 cm. Nothing in GEAR-VLA, SpatialVLA, 3DThinkVLA, or Spatial Forcing puts probability mass back on the commit mode. The one honest counter is SpatialVLA (2025)'s ablation shape — ego-3D encoding barely moves in-distribution success but drops variant-aggregation from 81.6 to 68.9 — which predicts our map's payoff appears in the 100-task tail, not on tasks we already solve. Since q denominates over all 100 tasks, that is a real defence, but it is a defence for a *tail* benefit, not for our stated bottleneck.

**Specific mechanism-level warnings.** IntentVLA's Put Spoon regressed 12.2 points against the frame-only baseline, with the authors' own explanation that "history may dilute attention to the small, thin spoon" — a memory tax levied on precisely our thin-object frontier, on unaliased tasks, with no gating mechanism offered. HAMLET's token-count sweep is non-monotonic and falls *below* the no-history baseline at n_m≥32 — a narrow good regime we would be searching on a different backbone, one shot, before a deadline. Robometer's own H3 ablation collapses to Kendall τ = **−0.14** (anti-correlated) when the 4B pretrained VLM is swapped for a scaled from-scratch transformer; our stage head is a ~0.1M 2-layer MLP, architecturally far closer to the condition that failed. History-conditioned BC's canonical failure is copycat/causal confusion — and our splice clips have **discontinuous history at the restore boundary**, so any memory module must mask/reset there or it trains on causally invalid transitions and silently nulls the result. Our stall-kick corpus makes this worse, not better: recent visual history in those clips is by construction "the robot was failing."

**Evidence quality across the entire survey is below our own bar.** No seeds and no confidence intervals in SEAL, Robometer, Cosmos Policy, SPARR, GEAR-VLA, IntentVLA, or HAMLET. SEAL's "up to 15%" is a max over three configurations, one of which is *negative* (mean +5.3pp, and it loses to its own base policy on the smallest config). Cosmos's planning claim rests on ~26 trials with undisclosed N and undisclosed γ. SPARR's two secondary ablations fail pooled significance (p≈0.055, and the self-imitation mechanism made cycle time *worse* on half the tasks it was motivated by). GEAR-VLA's headline mechanism is never ablated in four ablation tables, and its Sec 3.1 contradicts its own Appendix A on where the action expert attends. IntentVLA's largest effect lives entirely on a self-built benchmark with no component ablation; stripped of it, its independent gains are +2.1 to +7.6 with a 12-point regression and n=50 real-world at p≈0.09. Robometer's offline-RL table reports the best checkpoint across a γ sweep — selection on the evaluation metric, the exact failure our G1 lesson names. **Treat all of this as hypothesis generation. None of it is support.**

**Calendar.** ~10 weeks. Run-2 evals start now, the 18-run RaC campaign is in flight, and the 4-task expansion has a 2–4 day pose-replay long pole — all on one A5000. A full SPARR-style RESFIT-A1 (3 arms × 3 seeds) is ~1–2 weeks of box time by itself. Everything in §3 competes with that. §2 items 1, 3, 4, 5, 8a cost essentially nothing and should not be traded against anything.

## 7. Discard pile

- **fVDB (2025)** — GPU sparse-voxel infrastructure; a rebuild of the map, not a patch, and 13–21 ms CPU is not currently our bottleneck.
- **VoxFormer (2023)** — validates depth-as-intermediate-only, which our depth-aux patch already does; no new capability.
- **DistillNeRF (2024)** / **PartField (2025)** — both are map/affordance *rebuilds*; PartField needs 3D shape input and its part-resolution claim is untested for button faces. Revisit only if the affordance head is the measured blocker after §2.
- **ChronoEdit (2026)** — the transient-reasoning-token trick is cute but speculative; prototype only if the critic line stalls.
- **DriveSuprim (2025)** — right pathology, wrong domain; read for coarse-to-fine filtering, build nothing.
- **V-JEPA 2 (2025) / VLA-JEPA (2026)** — 16 s/action planning and a full pretraining stage respectively; both are out of budget by orders of magnitude.
- **Ctrl-World (2025), WMPO (2025)** — generative-world-model-as-data-source is strictly dominated by OmniGibson plus our restore/stall-kick engines.
- **4D-Gaussian family (ManiGaussian 2024, MSGField 2024) and streaming reconstruction (CUT3R/StreamVGGT/TTT3R)** — couple to control only as offline supervision or scene rebuild; optimize reconstruction fidelity, not any control signal; our map already occupies that slot inside budget.
- **µVLA (2026), MemoryVLA (2026)** — in-backbone recurrence works only when memory *structure* is consistent across tasks; across 100 heterogeneous household tasks that condition almost certainly fails. Keep µVLA solely as the no-regression licence (LIBERO unchanged at 96.2% while POMDP tasks 0.42 → 0.84).
- **Scene graphs (2026)** — 0.19 s/frame is 10× our map budget; the *object-identity/persistence gap* it exposes is real and belongs in the SERF Run-3 item instead.
- **History-Aware Visuomotor Policy via Point Tracking (2026)** — SAM-2 + TAPIP3D is too heavy a dependency chain for Oct 16. Keep one idea: async tracking with point-drop augmentation (0.93 → 5.44 Hz), which is how we would ever run anything off the critical path.
- **mindmap (2026), 3D Diffuser Actor (2024), PointVLA (2025), LaMP (2026), BridgeVLA (2025), SpatialVLA (2025)** — all take depth or point clouds as *policy input*, which is illegal for us. Harvest three methodology items only: PointVLA's skip-block analysis for choosing *which* expert blocks the map should modulate, 3D Diffuser Actor's +10pp from making attention relative (we already have this via per-arm displacement), and LaMP's zero-init gated cross-attention as the safe default pattern for any new head.
- **AHEAD (2026)** — latency compensation is a plausible contributor to our terminal misses, but the mechanism is validated only on genuinely dynamic scenes; our scenes are quasi-static and cheaper fixes come first.
- **Robometer-4B as an online reward** — 8 frames per trajectory against 10k–140k-step BEHAVIOR episodes is meaningless temporal coverage, zero-shot transfer to a new domain measured at τ 0.44, and our eval box is one A5000 under a 25 GB cgroup. Offline curation/ranking only.
- **GEAR-VLA's gradient decoupling, latent action IDs, embodiment canonicalization** — un-ablated, 240×H200-scale, and moot at one embodiment respectively.
- **VGGT in any form** — a second 1 B encoder violates the 24 GB budget we proved in G1, and its own frozen-vs-absent ablation says it buys nothing frozen.
- **HAMLET's Stage-1 time-contrastive init** — priced by the paper's own ablation at +0.3 to +0.6 for a whole extra training stage; partly redundant with our staleness channel.
- **SEAL's GPT-4o verifier in the eval loop** — 7–10 s per query (hidden in their setup only because they can keep simulating while waiting), hundreds of dollars per sweep, an external dependency inside the control loop, and its two documented failure modes are gripper–object contact from wrist views and occlusion — our exact frontier. Offline labelling only.
