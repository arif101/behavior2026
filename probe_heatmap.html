Reading

  • Task progress is already there (R² ≈ 0.85, every layer). With no stage head, the representation strongly encodes where-in-the-task the policy is. This is the substrate the 2025 winner's stage-voting exploited from outside; our stage head will anchor it inside the network.
  • Gripper intent is near-ceiling everywhere (≈ 0.94). Expected — it is close to an input echo — but a useful positive control: the probe machinery works.
  • Current action rises with depth (0.51 → 0.59, peak layers 13–15). Motor content accumulates toward late layers — consistent with the sensory → workspace → motor stratification reported for LLMs (J-lens paper), and with why per-layer KV mixing into the action expert helps.
  • Future end-effector delta reads as absent (R² ≤ 0) — but the label is suspect: a 16-frame mean-delta at 30 Hz is noise-dominated on smooth trajectories. Redo with sim-state end-effector poses before concluding anything.
What this baseline is for. This is the "before" picture of the vanilla recipe. The decisive comparison arrives with (a) geometric targets — object 6-DoF pose from the raw sim states (does the policy carry metric scene knowledge, or only semantics?) — and (b) the same map recomputed on the grounded-point + stage-head model. Architecture effects then show up as probe deltas, not vibes.

Caveats, honestly

  • Flatness across layers may partly be an artifact of mean-pooling the prefix positions; per-token readouts could reveal structure the pooling washes out.
  • These first targets are the easy ones; none of them yet tests geometry, which is the question that matters for our thesis.
  • Single checkpoint (5k steps), single task. Cross-checkpoint and cross-task maps come with the G3 infrastructure.
