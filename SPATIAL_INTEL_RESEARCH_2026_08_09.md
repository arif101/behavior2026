# Spatial-intelligence deep research v2 (2026-08-09)

Extends the 2026-08-08 survey. Ten papers deep-read and skeptic-verified since. **Zero survived verification at `run3_candidate` or above**: nine downgraded to `watch`, one to `discard`. That result is the report's headline, and Section 5 explains why it is a property of the literature rather than of the reviewers.

## 1. Landscape delta since 08-08

**Latent geometry.** No movement toward us; one paper moved away. SBP (ICRA 2026, 2510.03885) is the only new latent-map entry and it was already adjudicated and CUT in `FOVEATED_MEMORY_SPEC_v1.md` (2026-07-29) on a broader evidence base eleven days before this sweep surfaced it. Its headline "+15%" is Table II ID only — tabletop, PPO, single-arm — while its title says mobile manipulation; on the actual mobile benchmark the gains are +2/+2, +3/+5, +0/+5 points and its own naive-3D-lifting baseline ("Uplifted", no persistent map, no learned grid, no pretrained decoder) ties or beats Map-BC in every cell. Its Algorithm 1 suspends map updates while grasping, i.e. the map is structurally blind at exactly the contact moment our failure lives in. SCDP (2606.14535) is the one genuinely new geometric idea — sample image-pyramid features at the pixels the action chunk is heading toward, recomputed inside the denoising loop — but its own ablation prices the advertised closed loop at 6.7pp (s=0 static gripper foveation 75.8 vs full 82.5), i.e. ~92% of the effect is "look at multi-scale pixels under the gripper," which is approximately what our validated affordance-point → AdaLN route already does. The 08-08 conclusion stands unchanged: latent-geometry-via-AdaLN is convergent and already validated on our own base model.

**4D latent + perception.** The convergence is now unambiguous on *pattern* and unhelpfully vague on *mechanism*. Four independent 2026 papers — Fast-WAM (2603.16666), WAM4D (2606.14048), OneWM-VLA (2605.07931), plus also-swept GEM-4D and 4D-RGPT — all instantiate "dense geometric/future prediction as a training-only objective on the shared trunk, branch deleted at inference, zero eval latency." That is precisely our component (c). The field has independently converged on the pattern we already ship, which is reassuring and buys us nothing new. What it does *not* supply is any isolating evidence for the mechanism: not one of the four runs a non-geometric dense-target control, so "geometric priors transfer into the trunk" is nowhere distinguished from "any dense auxiliary plus extra training-time capacity regularizes a large model early in training." WAM4D adds a specific, useful negative: its own bi-directional row — geometry readable by the action stream, which our rules forbid — has the *best* action success in the placement table (76.6/72.5 vs 75.2/69.7), so the legality-mandated branch-deleted design is defended by the paper's own data as a cost to action accuracy, not a benefit.

**Precision mechanics.** Still the area where the literature is ahead of us, and it got sharper on diagnosis and no better on our specific blocker. ResFiT (2509.19301) describes its real-world base failure verbatim as "hovering and missed grasps with the dexterous hand" and takes 14% → 64% in ~15 minutes of robot data — our failure mode in the paper's own words. DAWN (2602.10539) supplies the missing critic engineering (base-policy-anchored replay warmup; LayerNorm on the critic, *not* the actor). Foresight Residual RL (2607.16506) supplies a measurement that should reshape our ResFiT spec: per-subtask residual RL lifts every subtask above 91% and lands full-task at 54.5%, i.e. **0.4pp below undecomposed end-to-end π0 at 54.9%**, and their chained BC (41.4) *loses 13.5pp* to end-to-end (54.9). Stage decomposition is a net negative until handoff quality is controlled. But the whole family shares one precondition we fail: every task in ResFiT starts at ≥14% base success, WEAVER selects tasks with base ≥20%, SPARR seeds from ~20 base successes, DAWN presupposes "an already capable base policy." Our frozen pre-registration records 1/31 (3.2%) primary and **0/25 on the commit probe**. Additive residuals and re-rankers operate within base support; at zero mass they have nothing to re-rank.

**World action models.** Fast-WAM closes the question the 08-08 survey opened, as a clean null in our favour. Deleting test-time future imagination costs 0.4–1.9 points (RoboTwin 91.8 vs 91.3 IDM vs 90.6 Joint; LIBERO 97.6 vs 98.0 vs 98.5 — note Joint actually *beats* Fast-WAM on LIBERO) while cutting latency >4x (190ms vs 810ms/~900ms). Deleting train-time video co-training costs 4.1–8.0 points. We could never have violated the first finding — a 6B video DiT at ~0.5–0.7s/chunk on an A5000 was never entering our control loop — and the second arm is confounded with destruction of the Wan2.2 pretrained video prior, which our PaliGemma trunk does not have to lose. WEAVER (2606.13672) reinforces the scoping trap from the cost side: 4xH100 x 10 days pretrain, OOD FID 23.5–30.8, 4.78s per rollout, and its "+38%" decomposes by the paper's own text to a +11pp marginal world-model contribution over real-data finetuning at n=20 (binomial 95% CI ≈ ±22pp). We have a ground-truth simulator that dominates a learned world model on both fidelity and cost. The 08-08 verdict holds: import the training-side pattern, never the model.

### 2.2 Amend `AUGUST_EXECUTION_SPEC.md` §5 before the ResFiT trainer is written. Zero compute.
**Component:** (d) stage head → chunk-critic → ResFiT.
Five spec edits, all one-liners at build time and expensive to retrofit after.

- **§5.3 — anchor the replay buffer with frozen-base-policy transitions, not (only) reward-labelled demos.** DAWN's Fig 3 measures Q-grounding error `|Q(s,a_base) − G^{π_base}|` over the first 100k steps: without a base-policy anchor it dips then *diverges* (PegInsertionSide ~2.5 → 17.5; TurnFaucet ~4 → 14); with it, it drops and stays low. The mechanism is that the critic must be accurate on the base policy's own state distribution, and demos come from a better policy that does not visit that region — the same off-manifold concern that `PHASE2_PLAN.md:56` used to ban residual RL outright, now given a measurable diagnostic and a cheap fix. DAWN's own framing (verbatim): "rather than requiring pre-collected demonstrations with reward labels, simply collecting transitions from the base policy is sufficient." Near-zero cost for us — we already generate frozen-policy rollouts for eval; keep them with sparse reward labels.
- **§5.5/5.6 — ban exploration noise during the anchor phase.** DAWN Fig 15: Gaussian σ=0.1 during anchor collection "causes PegInsertionSide to collapse entirely"; base-policy-only collection wins or ties every alternative. Whatever we do with contact-gated or spatially-directed exploration, scope it strictly post-anchor.
- **§5.2 — LayerNorm on every critic hidden layer, and NOT on the residual actor.** DAWN Fig 14 is the strongest falsifiable test in any of these ten papers: four arms (actor-none/critic-LN, actor-LN/critic-LN, actor-none/critic-HN, actor-HN/critic-HN) at fixed checkpoints. Actor-side normalization gives no improvement and slightly hurts; only critic-side helps (~2x `‖∇_{a_res}Q‖` on Peg). The scale-mismatch hypothesis predicts exactly that asymmetry, so this is a hypothesis that passed a test it could have failed. Second-order but real: LN keeps performance flat across λ ∈ [0.05, 0.4] (Fig 8), which de-risks choosing a *larger* λ — material for us, because a bounded λ·a_res may be structurally unable to close a 2.2cm gap.
- **§5.2 — make the critic asymmetric.** The critic never runs at eval, so it may legally consume GT sim state, GT depth, and object poses. Neither ResFiT nor DAWN does this (both feed actor and critic the same observation); under our rules it is free accuracy, same category as the component (c) depth aux. Enforce the boundary with an explicit input-schema assertion in the *actor's* forward pass — actor and critic train in one process and leaking a privileged tensor into the actor is a one-line accident.
- **§5.4 — reward terminal-state value, not stage completion; and do not adopt a learned multiplicative reward.** Foresight measures the pathology we were about to build: standard residual RL reaches Grasp 98.4 / Insert 92.2 / Rotate 99.2 and chains to 54.5% full-task, against 54.9% for the undecomposed policy. Under episode-end scoring, that failure would look excellent on our per-stage dashboards and move q by nothing. Take the diagnosis; **reject the fix's form** — a learned multiplicative reward model is neither potential-based nor policy-invariant, violating our own §5.4 mandate, and Foresight's Table III is scored by the same p_φ that PPO optimized against, so it cannot distinguish "produced better handoffs" from "found p_φ's adversarial region." Note also this confirms rather than extends: `SPATIAL_INTEL_RESEARCH_2026_08_08.md:55` already logged terminal-state quality as reproducible for free with our splice engine.

### 2.3 Add an anchor-degeneracy precheck to the RL pre-registration. Zero compute.
**Component:** (d).
**Mechanism.** DAWN's warmup mechanism presupposes value *variance* around the base. Under shifted sparse reward r ∈ {−1, 0} with a ~3%-success base, the MC return `G^{π_base}(s)` is near-constant for nearly all s, the grounding-error objective is trivially minimized by a constant critic, and the "value landscape around the base policy" the anchor is supposed to supply is flat. Consequence: `‖∇_{a_res}Q‖ ≈ 0` **with and without LayerNorm**, and any critic-sensitivity probe cannot distinguish reward degeneracy from h=32 chunk-width scale mismatch — it would return an uninterpretable result after burning contended A5000 hours.
**Action.** Pre-register, before any critic is fit: (i) MC-return variance across the anchor buffer must be non-trivial; (ii) the prior survey's pre-RL gate of ≥40 successful seed trajectories still applies; (iii) any critic-sensitivity probe carries a third arm using staged/dense reward or sim-restore-seeded near-commit states that actually produce *mixed* outcomes. Combine with the 08-08 support probe (PROCEED iff B ≥ 0.40 and B ≥ 3·p̂).

### 2.4 Log the action-parameterization result as confirmatory, and log its counter-citation. Zero compute.
**Component:** all — this is the output parameterization under every component.
**Finding.** 2602.23408 (13,000+ real rollouts, 500+ models, >16,000 A100-hours) recommends chunk-wise delta in joint space. We are already there and it is verified in code: `b1k.py` sets `needs_delta_comp=True` on torso/left_arm/right_arm; `config.py:417 _build_delta_mappings` wires those to `MappedDeltaActions`; `transforms.py:286` does `actions[..., action_indices] -= np.expand_dims(state[..., state_indices], axis=-2)` — one chunk-start state broadcast across the chunk. The mechanism is real (step-wise decoding integrates noise through a lower-triangular matrix, `O(k)` error growth vs `O(1)` for chunk-wise; Prop 4.1, confirmed by Fig 3(a) at ~10% on average).
**The honest reading, which the original assessment overstated.** The cell closest to us — bimanual, joint-space, generative/flow-matching — is a **null**: absolute 74.7±3.9 vs delta 74.6±5.2. The delta win is carried by single-arm scenarios. And the paper's *spatial* conclusion points away from our choice in our exact regime: under π0 LoRA fine-tuning, "task-space representations exhibit a more pronounced advantage and, in some cases, surpass joint-space control." So: log the temporal axis as externally confirmed; **do not** declare the spatial axis closed on this paper's authority, and log Fig 6(b) as the counter-citation any future EE-space proposal will use (3 coarse tasks, no numbers recoverable, confounded with cross-embodiment transfer we do not do).

### 2.5 Run the already-built, already-pre-registered `h28+blend` seam arm — and do not jump to h16.
**Component:** serving wrapper.
**Mechanism.** The action-space paper's horizon-coupling result (chunk-wise delta peaks at ~half its trained horizon because it is anchored to an increasingly stale chunk-start state; absolute peaks at full horizon) maps onto our measured seam pathology: arm-dim `|delta action|` at replan seams is 5–14x interior during the manipulation phase, pooled 8.99. Part of that is intrinsic re-anchoring, not purely flow-sample mode switching.
**Why not h16.** That extrapolation came from Fig 3(b), which reports *unlabelled curves with no numeric values* and is inference-time re-slicing of models trained at k=60. It is contradicted by two in-domain datapoints: the 2025 BEHAVIOR winner executes 26–28 of 32 on the same sim and robot class, and our only legal q=1.0 was full-chunk h32. Two in-domain observations beat one numberless out-of-domain curve.
**Also: fix the power.** At p ≈ 0.10–0.17, an n=5 arm has P(0 successes) ≈ 0.40–0.60, so a "≤ baseline at n=5" kill criterion manufactures a false-negative retirement of the question against documented >27% run-to-run variance. `patch_seam_blend.py` (K=4 linear cross-fade on the reserved overlap, grippers excluded, h32 path bit-identical) is written and pre-registered but **has no archived results**. Run that intermediate point at adequate n, on the checkpoint of record, with a matched h32 arm, before any further horizon extrapolation. Longer term, commit depth belongs to the chunk-critic per-state (adaptive prefix length, ACSAC 2026), not to a global constant.

### 2.6 Document the depth-aux label misalignment against the Run-2 readout. Hours.

## 5. Evidence-quality audit

Ten deep reads, ten downgrades. Grades: two C (WEAVER, OneWM-VLA; SBP also C), the rest B, none A.

**Max-over-config headlines, named.**
- **OneWM-VLA.** The abstract's "improves the average success rate from 47.9% to 61.3% on MetaWorld MT50" is not reconstructible from the paper's own Table 1. The string 47.9 appears **exactly once in the paper — in the abstract** — and equals no cell and no aggregation (π0 mean over H = 42.09, π0 best = 46.60). 61.3 is the H=5 maximum. Same-H is 43.97 → 61.28; average-over-H is 42.09 → 53.17. The abstract also benchmarks LIBERO-Long 95.6 against π0's 85.2 rather than π0.5's 92.4.
- **WAM4D.** 93.8 is the clean column. The average column reads WAM4D 91.8 = Fast-WAM 91.8 < LingBot-VA 92.3, and WAM4D is worst of the three on randomized (89.9 vs 91.8/91.6).
- **WEAVER.** "+38%" is base 0.44 → mixed 0.82. The paper's own text concedes synthetic-only is 4pp *below* real-only and that combining adds "+11% over real-data finetuning alone." The world model's marginal contribution is +11pp at n=20, where the binomial 95% CI on p=0.44 is ≈ ±22pp. Internal inconsistencies: planning gain quoted as 14% (abstract) and "15% average, max up to 20%" (body); speedup quoted as both "5–10x" and "~20x."
- **DAWN.** Two extractable numbers, "~5x" aggregated and "~6x to 90% on PegInsertionSide" (single hardest task). The ~5x aggregate covers only the six **state-observation** configs; both visual configs appear in no quotable number and in no component ablation — i.e. the RGB-only actor regime our legality rules force on us contributes to nothing citable.
- **SBP.** "+15%" is Table II ID (tabletop, PPO, single-arm) under a title and framing that say mobile manipulation, where the real deltas are +2/+2, +3/+5, +0/+5.
- **Action Space.** "Consistently across all platforms" is contradicted by the paper's own Table 1 in at least four per-task cells (bimanual Joint/DP 74.6 vs 74.7; multi-task Cube Joint/ACT 88.8 vs 94.4; multi-task Cube EE/DP 93.2 vs 96.4; multi-task Pick-Place Joint/ACT 72.8 vs 73.8). Consistency holds only after averaging.
- **Action Space, metric.** Progress score awards 0.5 for "aiming the correct direction" and 0.75 for a corner. Delta's largest margins land on the coarsest tasks (Pick Cup EE/ACT 63.1 → 97.9). BEHAVIOR q is goal-predicate satisfaction at episode end with transient progress scoring zero — the two metrics reward opposite things at exactly the axis in question.

**Missing ablations, by paper.** Seven of ten never ablate the component they ask you to import.
- Fast-WAM: no attention maps, no linear probes, no feature analysis anywhere — "video prediction improves world representations" rests entirely on downstream success deltas and is indistinguishable from generic auxiliary regularization. No random-init-trunk control to separate representation-building from pretrained-prior preservation.
- WEAVER: no reward-head ablation (never compared against decoding pixels and calling RoboMeter — not on accuracy, not on latency); no critic ablation at all (no γ/λ sweep, no no-bootstrap arm, no drop-V arm); and **no unfiltered-BC control**, since every "real data" arm is already advantage-filtered by WEAVER's own heads. ρ=0.870 is reported without stating the number of (policy x task) points behind it; at a plausible ~10, the 95% CI spans roughly 0.55–0.97, on the same 5 tasks the world model was finetuned on.

Table 8's H=20 "full" at 58.09 cannot be reconciled with Table 1 (52.10 at H=10, 46.16 at H=25), so the ablation's reference model is a different run. Token-count sweep confounded by a fixed 30k-step / 14.71M-LoRA budget, which the authors concede; the accompanying 37x FPS collapse over 33 added tokens signals an implementation pathology, not a representation principle.
- SCDP: no frozen-trajectory control (sample along the first iterate, not recomputed per denoising step), no SCDP-plus-wrist-camera condition anywhere, no calibration-error sensitivity.
- WAM4D: no non-geometric dense-target control, and the ablation is not parameter-matched (5.690–5.841B vs 5.089B). No current-vs-future depth comparison exists, so the "4D" in the title is unsupported.

Frozen-vs-unfrozen base never run. The a_base-conditioned actor and `Q(s, a_base + π(s,a_base))` parameterization, the one genuinely novel choice, never ablated against vanilla `Q(s,a)`.

**Statistics.** Eight of ten report no seeds, no CIs, and no significance tests anywhere. **Two contain no numerical results tables at all** — ResFiT's sim results exist only as Figure 4/5 curves ("200x", "near-perfect", "collapse to zero" are all read off plots), and DAWN's entire results section is curves and unlabelled bar charts. DAWN is the only paper with declared statistical discipline (8 seeds, 95% CI, stated as a paper-wide convention) and its numbers are unextractable from shaded regions. WAM4D's internal swing bounds its own noise floor near 5pp (Random-DA3-init scores 70.0 clean, *below* No-Depth's 71.7, but 74.8 randomized, *above* 69.1) — larger than every placement-table SR delta it asks you to act on. Action Space's ± are SEs over 3 rollout-trials of apparently one trained model per cell; the "500+ models" are spent on sweep breadth, not per-cell replication.

**The regime-selection pattern, which is the most important finding in this section.** WEAVER selects tasks "where the base policy achieves at least 20% success." ResFiT's tasks all start ≥14%. SPARR seeds from ~20 base successes. DAWN presupposes "an already capable base policy" and its residual "reduces the problem from policy search to local refinement around a reliable prior." Foresight's backward induction bootstraps from a working final-phase policy. Our frozen pre-registration records primary baseline 1/31 (3.2%) and commit probe 0/25. **The entire selection/residual/sharpening literature is measured in a regime defined by exclusion of ours**, and the mechanism most of it depends on — re-ranking or refining within base support — is structurally unable to fire at zero probability mass. This is not a transfer risk to be discounted; it is a precondition failure that should be checked before any of it is funded.

**Our own pipeline error, disclosed.** The SCDP deep-read asserted, and claimed to have verified against the paper's text, that no "DP + multi-scale F1–F5" baseline exists — after a first pass "hallucinated" one at ~81.0. Verification found the paper's Fig 4(b) paragraph says the opposite, verbatim: "Peak performance reaches 82.5% when all scales (F1–F5) are utilized, representing a 13.9%p improvement over the F5-only variant. Notably, even DP benefits substantially from using all feature levels." The discarded ~81.0 was plausibly a correct figure read, and its value is the single most decision-critical number in that assessment: if DP-with-pyramid is near 81, SCDP's marginal gain over "give the policy a feature pyramid" is ~1.5pp on 6 tasks and the item is a discard rather than a watch. Two process consequences: a claimed verification that reverses a prior extraction needs the quoted sentence attached, and any number read off a figure must be recorded as such rather than silently dropped.

## 6. Discard pile

- **SBP (2510.03885)** — already adjudicated and CUT in `FOVEATED_MEMORY_SPEC_v1.md` (2026-07-29) on a broader base including SERF; its OOD protocol pre-fits a latent map of the novel scene (illegal/infeasible at our eval); the surviving idea (backproject SigLIP patch features into voxel cells) is already spec'd as our L0 and attributed to SERF, not SBP; and its map suspends updates during grasping.
- **Test-time future imagination, as a class** — Fast-WAM's own null: 0.4–1.9pp for >4x latency. Confirms the 08-08 world-action-model scoping trap; closed.
- **Fast-WAM's architecture** — 6B Wan2.2 video DiT + video VAE at 190ms/chunk on an RTX 5090D projects to ~0.5–0.7s on our A5000. Engineering exclusion, not legality.
- **WEAVER's learned world model** — 4xH100 x 10 days pretrain, OOD FID 23.5–30.8, 4.78s/rollout; our simulator dominates it on fidelity and cost, and replaces its noisiest input with an exact one.
- **Advantage-filtered self-distillation at our base rate** — a sharpening operator over the policy's own samples, with no exploration mechanism; at 0/25 commit-probe mass the filter retains the empty set and the method silently no-ops. Restoring *state* changes the state distribution, not the action distribution.
- **On-policy PPO residual RL (Foresight)** — 128 parallel Isaac Gym envs, 310 iterations, no hardware or wall-clock disclosed; already superseded by `AUGUST_EXECUTION_SPEC.md` §5.1's off-policy choice (~200x sample efficiency, 200k vs 40M steps).
- **OneWM's one-token compression** — budget-confounded by the authors' own concession, and its frozen-backbone premise breaks under our fine-tuned trunk (the "free" target becomes a moving BYOL-style one requiring stop-gradient/EMA machinery the paper never validates).
- **SCDP's per-denoising-step closed loop** — s=0 static gripper foveation captures 75.8 of 82.5; the remainder is backbone-bound to conv pyramids (ResNet-18 82.5 vs ViT-S/16 31.7) and would require a from-scratch side encoder with zero evidence at our backbone.
- **WAM4D's full register branch** — 3–5 eng-days, +0.6B training params, +10–20% step time, real HBM pressure on a trainer already carrying four simultaneous Run-2 changes, against a single-seed 10-task 10k-step non-parameter-matched measurement.
- **Re-taping the depth-aux head to a mid-trunk layer as a cheap WAM4D proxy** — π0.5's shared prefix/suffix stack means this puts the geometry gradient *onto* the features the action expert reads, inverting the paper's stated mechanism; and `nn.scan` with `nothing_saveable` means even the probe is not cheap.
- **Fast-WAM's "free config audit"** — a no-op. The property it looks for is satisfied by construction: our depth head is a per-image-token `Linear(width→64)→gelu→Linear(64→1)` readout that emits no tokens and is loss-inert without labels.
- **h16 execution horizon** — extrapolated from a numberless figure at a k=60 training horizon, contradicted by the 2025 winner (26–28 of 32) and by our only q=1.0 (full-chunk h32).
- **Declaring the spatial (joint vs task space) action axis closed** — the paper cited to close it says the opposite in our fine-tuning regime.
- **Critic-based checkpoint selection on GT-trained targets** — legal, but a validity trap: it selects the checkpoints the critic likes. Keep offline policy evaluation as a triage tool, never as the selection rule.
